# Buyer-segment landing-page tests

The transcript suggests creating a base landing page, adapting its headline and reasons to buyer groups, and matching each ad to its page. Growth OS now has seven draft segments across the five companies. These are hypotheses drawn from the existing company briefings. No customer interviews or new reviews have been collected for them.

Use this sequence:

1. Start with Cloud Forest parents. Keep the approved $40 parent pilot as the current baseline. Confirm which booked visits can be traced to it and check whether those visitors attend.
2. Collect three anonymized inquiries or consenting parent interviews as an initial discovery sample. Record the source and date. Identify the repeated uncertainty about a first visit. Three conversations can inform copy; they do not establish market demand.
3. Review the parent page draft in the segment desk. Add a current, approved class photo. Confirm schedule, visitor form, confirmation, and follow-up responsibility. Keep the free-visit offer consistent from ad to page to booking.
4. Deploy the reviewed variant on traincloudforest.com. The Growth OS preview is for review. Keep the existing booking system and preserve the campaign and variant tags through booking. Record confirmed bookings in the CRM, not just button clicks.
5. Propose one page test after reviewing the pilot. Keep the audience, creative, offer, and schedule consistent between arms. Random assignment is preferable when the site supports it. Sequential comparisons are directional because timing can affect results. The remaining $60 is held; this document does not release it.
6. Record comparable visitor cohorts, confirmed bookings, attended visits, paid enrollments, and spend. Review cost per enrollment against verified contribution margin and available class capacity before considering a larger budget. Zero customers means acquisition cost is unavailable, rather than zero.

DojoZeus is the next draft to validate with a consenting owner. Begin with the after-class office workload. Record a short demo with sample school data, then ask which task would make the product worth trying. Keep the contract-ending segment separate until supported imports and transition requirements have been checked. Keep the verified founding offer and card-required trial consistent on every surface.

Lyceum starts with a teacher who already has a lesson. RigBoss starts with a specific paperwork task. Mech Correct starts with a repair update. Verify each demonstrated workflow before publishing claims. These segments have no new outbound or paid authorization.

## What to keep from the transcript

Reuse one page structure, keep the offer fixed, and change the headline and supporting reasons for a buying situation. Start with actual customer language where permission allows its use. A short list of reasons is a format to test. Write original copy and use approved brand assets.

The claims about reaching a million dollars per month, outperforming 99% of advertisers, and listicles being universally best are unsupported by the supplied transcript. Generating 20 personas does not validate them. With this budget, splitting spend across 20 pages would leave little evidence for each. Customer value and conversion lift must be measured.

## Measurement and operating limits

The segment desk stores manual cumulative totals in this browser and supports JSON backup and restore. It has no analytics collector, CRM connector, randomized traffic router, ad executor, or automatic spending authority. Its result table describes the entered totals and never declares a statistical winner. Keep raw customer records in the CRM. Do not paste private reviews, names, email addresses, or payment details into this desk.

Before launch, record the changed variable, date range, primary conversion, result source, and the expected delay before a customer pays. Choose a test duration and sample requirement from baseline conversion data; that baseline is not available yet. Resolve broken tracking or signup before spending further. Avoid changing a running test because of a handful of clicks.

## Supporting guidance

Google describes the importance of relevant landing-page content and clear navigation in [its landing-page guidance](https://blog.google/products/ads-commerce/search-ads-and-the-importance-of-landing-page-navigation/). Its [experiment guide](https://services.google.com/fh/files/misc/campaign_experiments_two_sheeter.pdf) recommends focusing on one variable and one primary metric. These support the testing approach; they do not prove the transcript's revenue claims or predict results on Meta.
